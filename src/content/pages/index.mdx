## A mountain mesh with a personality.

Most Meshtastic sites look like equipment catalogs. This one should feel like the place it represents: **dark roads, steep ridgelines, radio glow, and people who know how to get things done.**

We're building a local knowledge base for:

- **Getting on the air** — choosing a node, configuring Meshtastic, and joining the local channel.
- **Building coverage** — repeaters, antennas, elevation, solar power, and practical field deployments.
- **Understanding the network** — how LoRa and Meshtastic actually move a message through the mountains.
- **Going off-grid** — using your node when the internet, cellular network, or power isn't available.
- **Building together** — local projects, node hosts, field tests, and community events.

## The Graham County advantage

Mountains make radio interesting.

A node that looks perfect on a workbench can behave completely differently once it's behind a ridge. Elevation, antenna placement, trees, weather, terrain, and the position of neighboring nodes all matter.

That makes Graham County a great place to learn **real-world mesh networking**.

## This is a local network.

The goal isn't to sell you another gadget.

The goal is to make **Graham County more connected when conventional connectivity isn't available** — and to make learning radio technology approachable, useful, and a little more fun.

> **Built in the mountains. Powered by people. Carried by radio.**

If you're in Graham County and want to help test coverage, host a node, build a repeater, or simply learn what Meshtastic can do, you're in the right place.
