# GET STARTED

## Your first node. Your first message. Your first mesh.

Meshtastic is easiest to understand when you stop thinking of it as a replacement for your phone and start thinking of it as a **local radio network you carry with you**.

### 1. Pick your role

**HANDHELD / CLIENT**  
Your everyday node. Carry it, connect your phone, and send messages through the mesh.

**VEHICLE**  
A mobile node with a good antenna and a useful position on the road.

**ROUTER / REPEATER**  
A fixed node placed high enough to hear and forward traffic for everyone else.

**SOLAR NODE**  
A router designed to live outside for long periods with solar power.

### 2. Pick compatible hardware

Look for a Meshtastic-supported LoRa device for the **US 915 MHz** band. Hardware changes quickly, so this site will focus on how to choose rather than pretending one device is always the best.

Prioritize:

- Reliable Meshtastic support
- USB-C or an easy programming connection
- A real external antenna connector when you need range
- Low power consumption for fixed nodes
- Weather-resistant enclosures for outdoor deployments

### 3. Install Meshtastic

Use the official Meshtastic documentation for the current firmware and app instructions.

**[Meshtastic documentation →](https://meshtastic.org/docs/)**

### 4. Configure before you climb a mountain

Give your node a useful name. Set the correct region. Configure your radio settings for the local network. Then test it at home before depending on it in the field.

> **Don't make your first configuration experiment on the side of a mountain with 12% battery left.**

### 5. Join the local network

This site will publish the Graham County channel information, local conventions, node directory, and community contacts as the network develops.

## What should I buy?

Start simple.

If you're brand new, a small handheld client is enough to learn the basics. You do **not** need to build a solar repeater on day one.

Once you understand the radio, that's when the fun starts.
