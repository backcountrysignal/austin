# FIELD GUIDE

## Radio is simple. The terrain isn't.

This is the practical side of Meshtastic.

### LINE OF SIGHT

More height usually beats more transmit power.

A node with a clear view from a ridge can outperform a much more expensive node sitting behind a hill.

### ANTENNAS

The antenna is part of the radio.

For fixed installations, prioritize a properly mounted outdoor antenna, quality coax, weather protection, and correct frequency. Keep feedline losses low.

### POWER

A repeater that dies every night isn't infrastructure.

For outdoor nodes, plan around the worst part of the season — not the perfect sunny afternoon when you installed it.

### WEATHER

Graham County means rain, humidity, temperature swings, and exposed mountain locations.

Outdoor electronics need an enclosure, cable management, strain relief, and a way to manage condensation.

### FIELD TESTING

Don't guess about range.

Take two nodes into the field. Record:

- Location
- Elevation
- Distance
- Antenna
- Node hardware
- Packet/message behavior
- Weather
- What was between the two radios

A good field report turns one person's experiment into everyone's knowledge.

## The golden rule

**Put the best antenna and the best location where the network needs them most.**

Not every node needs to be powerful. A well-placed node can do more for the mesh than a pile of mediocre nodes at ground level.
